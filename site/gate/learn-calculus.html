Calculus & Optimization — learn & intuition.

// what this page is

The picture behind limits, derivatives, Taylor series and optimization — plus how to solve each GATE question type with worked examples. Small syllabus, high accuracy expected: these are guaranteed marks once the rules are reflex. Pair with the cheatsheet for recall.

Calculus is the mathematics of change and accumulation. The derivative is an instantaneous rate — the slope of the curve at a point. Optimization is just finding where that slope is zero and checking what kind of turning point you've landed on. Everything here is a variation on those two moves.

01 Limits & continuity

key ideaA limit asks: as x creeps toward a, what value does f(x) approach? — regardless of what happens exactly at a. A function is continuous at a if that approached value equals f(a): no jump, no hole, no gap.

Formally continuous at a means: f(a) exists, the limit exists (left = right), and they're equal.

How to evaluate a limit

  1. Plug in. If you get a number, done.
  2. If 0/0 or ∞/∞ → indeterminate → apply L'Hôpital: differentiate top and bottom separately, retry.
  3. Algebra first when cleaner: factor/cancel, rationalize, or use a standard limit (sin x/x → 1).
workedlim(x→0) sin x / x = 0/0 → L'Hôpital → cos x / 1 → cos 0 = 1. (Or recall it as a standard limit.)

02 Differentiability — and why it implies continuity

think of it likeDifferentiable means the curve is smooth enough to have one well-defined tangent line at that point — no sharp corner, no break. |x| at x=0 is continuous (no gap) but not differentiable (a sharp corner — left slope −1, right slope +1 disagree).
the one-way implicationDifferentiable ⇒ continuous, but continuous ⇏ differentiable. If a curve has a tangent it can't be broken; but an unbroken curve can still have a corner. GATE tests this direction every year.

03 Derivative rules — make these reflex

  • Power: d/dx xⁿ = n·xⁿ⁻¹
  • Product: (uv)′ = u′v + uv′
  • Quotient: (u/v)′ = (u′v − uv′)/v²
  • Chain: d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))·g′(x) — differentiate outer, keep inner, times derivative of inner.
  • Standard: (eˣ)′=eˣ, (ln x)′=1/x, (sin x)′=cos x, (cos x)′=−sin x.
the #1 slipForgetting the chain rule's inner derivative. d/dx sin(2x) is 2cos(2x), not cos(2x). The ×g′(x) factor is where easy marks vanish. Whenever you differentiate "a function of a function," ask "what's inside, and what's its derivative?"

04 Taylor series — approximating any function with a polynomial

think of it likeZoom in on a smooth curve and it looks like a line (1st-order), then a parabola (2nd-order)… A Taylor series rebuilds a function near a point from its value and all its derivatives there. Each term corrects the previous approximation a little more.

f(x) = f(a) + f′(a)(x−a) + f″(a)(x−a)²/2! + …

Memorize the three standard expansions around 0 (Maclaurin) — they appear directly and inside limits:

  • eˣ = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + …
  • sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …
  • cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − …
workedlim(x→0) (sin x − x)/x³. Use sin x ≈ x − x³/6 → numerator ≈ −x³/6 → limit = −1/6. Series beats repeated L'Hôpital here.

05 Maxima & minima — the optimization routine

key ideaAt a smooth peak or valley the tangent is flat, so f′(x)=0. That gives candidate points; the second derivative tells you which kind: f″>0 = valley (min, curves up), f″<0 = peak (max, curves down).

The single-variable optimization recipe

  1. Find critical points: solve f′(x)=0 (and note where f′ doesn't exist).
  2. Classify with the second-derivative test: f″>0 → local min, f″<0 → local max.
  3. If f″=0, it's inconclusive → use the first-derivative sign change (or higher derivatives).
  4. For a closed interval [a,b]: also evaluate the endpoints — the global max/min may sit there, not at a critical point.

Fig — flat tangent (f′=0) marks turning points; curvature sign (f″) tells max from min.

the catchOn a closed interval, never forget the endpoints. A function can have its largest value at x=a or x=b even though f′≠0 there. Checking only the critical points is the classic lost mark.

06 The bridge to ML optimization

why this subject matters downstreamIn machine learning, training minimizes a loss function. The multivariable version of f′=0 is "gradient = 0"; the multivariable f″ is the Hessian, whose eigenvalue signs (definiteness) decide min vs max vs saddle. Gradient descent just walks downhill — repeatedly stepping opposite the slope. Single-variable intuition here is the seed of all of it.

07 The high-frequency exam traps

memorize these

  • Differentiable ⇒ continuous, not the reverse — |x| at 0 is the counterexample.
  • Chain rule inner derivative — d/dx f(g(x)) needs the ×g′(x).
  • Closed interval → check endpoints, not just f′=0 points.
  • f″=0 is inconclusive — fall back to first-derivative sign change.
  • L'Hôpital only for 0/0 or ∞/∞ — rearrange other indeterminates (0·∞, ∞−∞) into that form first.
  • Know eˣ, sin x, cos x series cold — they crack hard limits instantly.

08 FAQ

When should I use L'Hôpital vs Taylor series?

L'Hôpital for a single 0/0 that clears in one or two differentiations. Taylor series when repeated L'Hôpital gets messy (e.g. x³ in the denominator) — substitute the series and read off the leading term.

How do I classify a critical point if f″=0?

The second-derivative test fails. Use the first-derivative test: if f′ changes + to − it's a max, − to + it's a min, no sign change → inflection (neither).

Local vs global extremum?

Local = highest/lowest in a neighbourhood. Global = highest/lowest over the whole domain. On a closed interval the global extremes are among {critical points} ∪ {endpoints}; compare their f-values.

  • Derivative = instantaneous slope; optimization = where slope is zero + which way it curves.
  • Make the derivative rules and chain rule reflex — most lost marks are mechanical slips.
  • Closed interval ⇒ test endpoints too.
  • Know the eˣ/sin/cos Taylor series cold — they shortcut the hardest limits.
