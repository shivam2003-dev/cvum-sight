// what this page is

Fast methods for every GA question type — quantitative (percentages, ratios, mensuration, number series, data interpretation), verbal (grammar, vocabulary, comprehension), and logical/analytical reasoning — with worked examples. GA is 15 marks of the cheapest, most reliable points on the paper. Never skip it: a percentage shortcut takes 20 seconds and is worth the same as a hard ML question.

GA rewards speed with accuracy, not depth. The whole game is recognizing the question shape and applying a one-line method. Below is the toolkit: drill these until each is a reflex and GA becomes free marks that lift your overall score and rank.

01 Percentages, ratios & proportion

key ideaA percentage is just a fraction out of 100. "x% of y" = (x/100)·y. Successive changes multiply, never add: +20% then −20% = ×1.2×0.8 = 0.96 → a net 4% loss, not zero.
  • % increase = (new−old)/old × 100. % decrease uses old as the base.
  • Ratio a:b means parts; total = a+b parts. Scale by the part value.
workedPrice rises 25%, then falls 20%. Net = 1.25×0.80 = 1.00 → back to original. Recognizing the multiply rule avoids the "+5%" trap.
the catchPercentage points ≠ percent. A rate going from 10% to 12% is +2 percentage points but a +20% relative increase. GATE words this to trip you.

02 Speed, time, work & averages

  • distance = speed × time. Average speed for equal distances = harmonic mean 2ab/(a+b), not (a+b)/2.
  • Work: if A finishes in a days, A's rate = 1/a per day; combine rates by adding. Together: 1/(1/a+1/b).
  • Average = sum/count; adding a value above the average pulls it up.
workedGo at 40, return at 60 (same distance). Average = 2·40·60/(40+60) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h, not 50.

03 Mensuration & geometry

  • Rectangle area = l·w; circle area = πr², circumference = 2πr.
  • Triangle area = ½·base·height; Pythagoras a²+b²=c².
  • Cuboid volume = l·b·h; cylinder = πr²h; sphere = (4/3)πr³.
the catchScaling. Double every length → area ×4, volume ×8. Many GA questions hinge on this square/cube scaling, not on re-deriving the formula.

04 Number & letter series

How to crack a series

  1. Check differences between consecutive terms (arithmetic?).
  2. Check ratios (geometric?).
  3. Check squares/cubes/primes, or two interleaved series.
  4. For letters, convert to positions A=1…Z=26 and treat as numbers.
worked2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? Differences 4,6,8,10 → next diff 12 → 42. (These are n²+n.)

05 Data interpretation

key ideaDI tests reading a table/chart/graph, not hard math. Read the axes and units first, find the exact cells the question needs, then do one percentage or ratio. Don't compute numbers you don't need.

How to attack a DI set

  1. Read the title, axis labels, and units before any number.
  2. For each sub-question, locate only the relevant data points.
  3. Estimate first — eliminate far-off options, then compute precisely if needed.

06 Verbal ability — grammar & vocabulary

  • Subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, pronoun reference are the staple error types. Read the whole sentence; find what the verb's true subject is.
  • Vocabulary: build synonym/antonym pairs; use word roots (bene- good, mal- bad) to decode unknowns.
  • Word analogies: name the precise relationship in the first pair, then apply it.
worked — analogy"Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?" Relationship = professional : workplace → School. State the relation in words first; it prevents the tempting-but-wrong option.

07 Reading comprehension

How to read for marks

  1. Skim the questions first so you know what to hunt for.
  2. Read the passage for structure (claim, support, conclusion), not every word.
  3. Answer only from the text — never from outside knowledge or assumptions.
  4. For "inference" questions pick the option the passage must support, not the strongest-sounding one.

08 Logical & analytical reasoning

  • Syllogisms ("All A are B…") — draw Venn diagrams; a conclusion holds only if true in every valid diagram.
  • Seating / ordering puzzles — make a grid, fill in hard constraints first, branch on the rest.
  • Blood relations / directions — sketch a tree or a compass; don't track it in your head.
the catch"Possibly true" vs "must be true." A syllogism conclusion is valid only if it holds in all consistent arrangements. One counter-diagram kills it. Mixing these up is the classic reasoning error.

09 How to play GA in the exam

The GA game plan

  1. Do GA early while fresh — it's cheap, reliable marks (5×1 + 5×2 = 15).
  2. One pass: solve the instant ones, flag the slow puzzles, come back.
  3. Estimate to eliminate options before full computation.
  4. Never leave GA for the last 10 minutes — that's where rank is quietly lost.

10 The high-frequency exam traps

memorize these

  • Successive % changes multiply, never add; +x% then −x% is a net loss.
  • Percentage points ≠ percent.
  • Equal-distance average speed = harmonic mean, not arithmetic.
  • Scaling: length ×k ⇒ area ×k², volume ×k³.
  • Syllogism: "must be true" needs ALL diagrams; one counterexample disproves.
  • RC answers from the text only — no outside knowledge.
  • Read DI units/axes first; compute only what's asked.

11 FAQ

Is GA worth serious prep if it's "only" 15 marks?

Absolutely. It's 15% of the paper at the lowest difficulty and highest reliability. A top rank is built on never dropping easy marks — GA is the easiest 15 on the paper.

How do I get faster?

Drill the standard methods (percentage, ratio, average speed, series) until they're reflex, and practise timed DI sets. Speed comes from instant pattern recognition, not from re-deriving formulas.

When in the exam should I do GA?

Early, while you're fresh and calm. Banking the reliable GA marks first reduces pressure on the technical sections later.

  • GA = cheapest, most reliable 15 marks — never skip, do it early.
  • Win on speed via pattern recognition: each question type has a one-line method.
  • Watch the staples: % multiply not add, harmonic-mean speed, k²/k³ scaling, "must be true" in reasoning.
  • For verbal/RC/DI: answer strictly from what's given, eliminate options by estimation.
