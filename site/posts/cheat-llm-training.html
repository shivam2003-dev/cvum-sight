LLM Training — The Scaling Cheatsheet.

training distributed fsdp fine-tuning
Training at scale is a memory and communication problem before it's a math problem. You spend VRAM on weights + gradients + optimizer states + activations, and you spend wall-clock on moving gradients between GPUs. Every technique below is buying back one of those two.

1. Where the VRAM goes

ConsumerRough cost (Adam, mixed precision)
Weights2 bytes/param (BF16)
Gradients2 bytes/param
Optimizer statesAdam: 8–12 bytes/param (FP32 momentum + variance + master weights)
ActivationsScales with batch × seq_len × layers — often the biggest, and the most controllable

Hence ~16–18 bytes/param just for states with Adam. A 7B model needs ~110GB+ before activations — which is why sharding (ZeRO/FSDP) exists.

2. Parallelism strategies

StrategySplitsWhen
Data parallel (DDP)The batch; full model replicated per GPUModel fits on one GPU; scale throughput
ZeRO / FSDPOptimizer states → grads → params (stages 1/2/3)Model too big to replicate; default for large training
Tensor parallelEach layer's matmuls across GPUsHuge layers; needs NVLink (intra-node)
Pipeline parallelLayers into stagesCross-node; mind the bubble
Sequence/context parallelThe sequence dimensionVery long context

3D parallelism = TP × PP × DP, used for the largest models. FSDP alone covers most sub-100B fine-tuning needs.

3. ZeRO / FSDP stages

  • Stage 1 — shard optimizer states across GPUs. Cheapest, big memory win for Adam.
  • Stage 2 — also shard gradients.
  • Stage 3 — also shard parameters; each GPU holds only a slice, all-gathers layers on demand. Max memory savings, more communication.
  • Offload — push states/params to CPU or NVMe when even sharding isn't enough (slower).
sharding trades memory for comms ZeRO-3 lets you fit enormous models but all-gathers params every layer. If your interconnect is slow (PCIe, cross-node without fast fabric), comms dominates and GPUs idle. Match the stage to your hardware; prefer a lower stage that still fits.

4. Memory-saving techniques

  • Mixed precision (BF16) — half the weight/grad/activation bytes; BF16 avoids loss scaling.
  • Gradient (activation) checkpointing — don't store activations; recompute in backward. ~√N memory for ~33% more compute. Huge for long sequences.
  • Gradient accumulation — big effective batch without big VRAM; accumulate over micro-batches before step().
  • Fused optimizers, 8-bit Adam (bitsandbytes) — shrink optimizer states.
  • FlashAttention — removes the O(N²) attention memory.

5. Training stability

  • Loss scaling (FP16 only) — scale loss up before backward to stop gradient underflow; dynamic scaler backs off on overflow.
  • Gradient clipping — clip global grad norm (e.g. 1.0) to survive spikes.
  • Warmup + cosine decay — ramp LR over the first steps, then decay. Skipping warmup causes early divergence.
  • Z-loss / qk-norm / careful init — large-model stabilizers against logit blow-up.
  • Watch grad norm as your early-warning signal — a spike precedes a NaN.
loss goes to NaN — usual suspects LR too high, no warmup, no grad clipping, FP16 overflow (use BF16 or loss scaling), bad data (corrupt sample, inf in inputs), or division by zero in a custom layer. Bisect: lower LR, clip, switch to BF16, check the batch at the failing step.

6. Fine-tuning (PEFT)

MethodWhat
Full fine-tuneUpdate all weights. Best quality, most VRAM, risk of forgetting.
LoRAFreeze base, train small low-rank adapters (A·B). ~0.1–1% params trained; mergeable at inference.
QLoRA4-bit quantized frozen base + LoRA. Fine-tune big models on one GPU.
Adapters / prefix / IA³Other parameter-efficient variants.

7. Post-training / alignment

  • SFT — supervised fine-tune on instruction/response pairs. The foundation of "chat".
  • RLHF (PPO) — train a reward model from preferences, optimize the policy against it. Powerful, complex, unstable.
  • DPO — direct preference optimization; skips the reward model, optimizes preferences directly. Simpler, popular default.
  • Others — ORPO, KTO, GRPO (reasoning/RLVR). The space moves fast; DPO-family is the pragmatic baseline.

8. Data & checkpointing

  • Data is the product. Dedup, quality-filter, decontaminate against eval sets, mix domains deliberately. Garbage in scales to garbage out.
  • Sequence packing — concatenate samples to fill context, avoid padding waste.
  • Checkpoint often — long runs WILL hit a node failure. Save optimizer state too; support resume. Async/sharded checkpointing avoids stalls.
  • Log loss, grad-norm, LR, tokens/sec, MFU (model FLOPs utilization) — MFU tells you how much of the hardware you're actually using.

9. Quick reference

VRAM ≈ params×(2+2+~12) + activations   # weights+grad+Adam states
Fit bigger: BF16 + checkpointing + accumulation + FSDP/ZeRO(+offload)
Stability: warmup + cosine, clip grad-norm 1.0, BF16, watch grad-norm
Fine-tune cheap: QLoRA (4-bit base + LoRA)
Align: SFT → DPO (or PPO/GRPO)
Health: tokens/sec, MFU, grad-norm, eval loss
Resume: checkpoint weights+optimizer; expect failures

10. Interview Q&A

  • Where does training memory actually go?Weights + gradients + optimizer states (Adam ≈ 12 bytes/param) + activations. With Adam in mixed precision it's ~16–18 bytes/param before activations — which is why we shard and checkpoint.
  • DDP vs FSDP/ZeRO?DDP replicates the full model per GPU and all-reduces grads — needs the model to fit. FSDP/ZeRO shards optimizer states/grads/params so each GPU holds a slice — fits much bigger models, at the cost of more communication.
  • What do the ZeRO stages shard?Stage 1: optimizer states. Stage 2: + gradients. Stage 3: + parameters (all-gather per layer on demand). Higher stage = less memory, more comms.
  • How does gradient checkpointing help?It discards activations in forward and recomputes them in backward — roughly √N memory for ~33% more compute. Essential for long sequences / large models.
  • BF16 vs FP16 for training stability?BF16 keeps FP32's exponent range, so no loss scaling and far fewer overflow NaNs. FP16 needs dynamic loss scaling. Default to BF16 on Ampere+.
  • Loss spiked to NaN — how do you debug?Check LR/warmup, add grad clipping, switch to BF16, inspect grad-norm before the spike, and audit the batch at that step for corrupt/inf inputs. Bisect by lowering LR.
  • LoRA vs full fine-tuning — when?LoRA/QLoRA when you want cheap, fast, mergeable adaptation with limited VRAM and lower forgetting risk. Full fine-tune when you need maximum quality and have the compute.
  • DPO vs RLHF/PPO?PPO trains a reward model then optimizes a policy against it — powerful but complex and unstable. DPO optimizes preferences directly without a reward model or RL loop — simpler and a strong default.
  • What is MFU and why track it?Model FLOPs Utilization — achieved FLOPs ÷ hardware peak. It tells you how efficiently you're using the GPUs; low MFU means comms/starvation/bad kernels, not a model problem.
  • Why checkpoint optimizer state, not just weights?Adam's momentum/variance define the trajectory; resuming from weights alone restarts the optimizer and disrupts training. Save both for a clean resume after the inevitable node failure.
