Inference Engineering, Monthly 2026.

ml inference vllm sglang

This page is the running log of inference engineering for 2026. Each month gets one section. The newest month sits at the top, and each section stays collapsed until you open it. Every section follows the same shape: the important changes in the engines, the concepts that explain them, practical checks, and reading links. Release notes are the source for what shipped. Anything beyond the notes is interpretation, and I mark it that way.

September 2026 the runner becomes the only path, correctness bugs in LoRA and cache sizing, decision models in Ollama, operational hardening in llm-d

Summary: September closed the year's arc with consolidation and with correctness work.

The year ends with a default that is also a test

Across the year, a steady number of features became defaults: asynchronous scheduling in January, speculative decoding version 2 in May, the cache manager version 2 in August, and now the runner for every model in September.

A default is also a test.

The lesson for a team at the end of the year is to treat the default as a moving target.

Model Runner V2 for every model

vLLM v0.29.0 on September 9 brought 594 commits from 277 contributors and completed the rollout of Model Runner V2 to all models.

Batch-sharded sampling deserves a closer look, because it is an example of the memory budget from January applied to a part of the pipeline most people do not think about.

The CUDA graph memory profiling is the other half of the same story.

vLLM v0.30.0 on September 22 brought 762 commits from 315 contributors, the largest count of the year.

Correctness bugs in the release candidates

TensorRT-LLM's September candidates disclosed bugs that are worth a close read, because they are the kind that do not fail a test suite.

A silent adapter bug is a serious one.

The memory estimate bug is the opposite kind of problem.

The rc26 notes also mentioned GPT-OSS with an Eagle draft model among the known issues.

TensorRT-LLM removes AutoDeploy

TensorRT-LLM v1.3.0rc29 on September 29 removed the AutoDeploy integration and its public entry points, labeled as a breaking change.

A removal that was announced in July and carried out in September is the pattern to expect from a mature project.

The same candidate added a long list of model items, including Qwen3.5 checkpoints with global FP8 scales, a vision rotary embedding change for Gemma 4 that avoids a matrix multiply, a CuTeDSL MoE backend for Kimi K3 NVFP4, and extended piecewise CUDA graphs for MiniMax-M3. The breadth of the list shows how much of the runtime is now shared across model families.

SGLang: Qwen3.8 and the 2.4-trillion-parameter model

SGLang v0.5.19 on September 5 brought 786 pull requests from 214 contributors, and its headline model was Qwen3.8 at 2.4 trillion total parameters with 95 billion active, listed in the notes as an autoregressive model with a dense 27 billion parameter sibling.

SGLang v0.5.20 on September 18 brought 713 pull requests and added GLM-5.3-Flash, Tencent's Hy4-Preview, and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next.

A large model with a small active set raises a specific question for serving: where do the weights live?

Ollama adds decision models

Ollama v0.35.0 on September 28 introduced decision models through a new endpoint called systemone.

This is a different kind of model from the ones earlier in the series.

The interface matters as much as the model.

A note of caution applies.

Ollama v0.34.x ran from September 5 to September 23, with several patch releases, and v0.33.3 on September 2 added image and audio input for Gemma 4 on the MLX engine, reported cached prompt tokens, and honored default parameters defined in GGUF files.

llm-d v0.10.0: operational hardening

llm-d v0.10.0 on September 29 set its themes in plain language: operational hardening, to make the production path safe and boring, with rollouts, high availability, and failure behavior that operators can trust, and production readiness, to move the flagship paths from working to default.

The word boring deserves attention.

The move of changes upstream is a sign of consolidation across the ecosystem.

The llm-d themes also match what a fleet team should watch for in production.

LMDeploy: DeepEP v2 and a Mooncake connector

LMDeploy v0.17.0 on September 1 integrated DeepEP v2, a communication library for mixture-of-experts dispatch, and added Kimi K2.6 support on its PyTorch path.

LMDeploy v0.18.0 on September 28 followed.

llama.cpp and the MoE reduction

llama.cpp published a build on September 1 with a CUDA change that fuses the weighted reduction of mixture-of-experts outputs.

Memory traffic is the constraint that matters most for decode, because each generated token must move the weights and the intermediate results.

The release cadence continued at a high pace in September, with a batch of builds on September 30 alone.

FlashAttention 4 reaches beta 33

The FlashAttention repository published fa4 betas 29 through 33 in September, with the latest on September 30. Beta 29 on September 2 was a compilation speedup for scalar mask generation on SM100 hardware.

That long beta period is itself a lesson about kernels.

Summary: August was the month the cache became something the router, the runtime, and the client all reason about together.

Ray Serve LLM: routing on KV state

Ray 2.58.0 on August 23 completed a feature that Ray 2.57 had previewed: KV cache and token aware request routing for Ray Serve LLM.

Kimi K3 and the hybrid attention pattern

SGLang v0.5.17 on August 8 described Kimi K3 day-zero support.

vLLM v0.27.0 on August 10 brought Kimi K3 support as a full stack in one release: core model files, kernels, Python and Rust frontends, and compressed-tensors quantized checkpoints.

TensorRT-LLM: KV cache manager V2 by default

TensorRT-LLM v1.3.0rc25 on August 31 announced that its KV cache manager V2 is now the default for a long list of model families, including DeepSeek V3, R1, and V3 Lite, DeepSeek V3.2 and V4, GLM-5, GPT-OSS, Mistral Large 3, the Kimi K2 line including K3, MiniMax M2 and M3, the Nemotron H family, and Qwen3-Next, Qwen3.5, and Qwen3.8. The candidate that preceded it had listed a KVCacheManagerV2 teardown failure among its known issues, which is a reminder that a default flip follows a period of known problems.

A default change in the cache manager reaches the most sensitive layer of the engine.

The breadth of the list is informative.

Mooncake and LMCache: the storage layer keeps moving

Mooncake v0.3.13 on August 26 and v0.3.13.post1 on August 31 added several fixes in its transfer engine and CI.

LMCache v0.5.5 release candidates arrived on August 28 and August 31, with builds for CUDA 12.9, ROCm, and Intel XPU alongside the standard wheel.

LMCache's ROCm wheel in early August, for AMD MI300X and MI350 series hardware, was built to match the upstream vLLM ROCm container's torch version.

The storage work in August also reflects a broader lesson about where failures hide.

llama.cpp reaches a release every few days

The llama.cpp project is the engine underneath much of local inference, and its build cadence matters more each month.

For a team, a fast cadence means that pinning a version is a choice, not a default.

The llama.cpp build cadence also explains why Ollama's engine changes track it.

xDiT and parallel diffusion

xDiT released version 0.5.0 on August 31. The project is a parallel inference framework for diffusion transformers, which splits the work of generating an image or video across several GPUs.

The general point is that the engines in this series now serve several kinds of model, and the lessons carry across.

Reading August against the year so far

Agreement is also where teams get hurt in ways that dashboards do not show.

DSpark: sizing the verify window by confidence

SGLang v0.5.16 on July 25 introduced DSpark, which the notes describe as confidence-driven speculative decoding.

DSpark drafts in blocks, semi-autoregressively, and then sizes each verification window from the draft's own confidence.

The idea of tying the window to confidence is a general one.

The release notes also mention an environment setting that changes how verification is batched in compact mode.

SGLang v0.5.15 on July 10 made Spec V2 the default, with zero-overhead scheduling through CUDA-graphable draft extension, removed device-to-host and host-to-device synchronizations, and fused metadata operations.

June 2026 peer-to-peer KV transfer, TensorRT backend sunset, MoE load balancing, FP8 cache in LMDeploy, multi-token drafting on Apple silicon

Summary: June was about moving cache between machines and retiring old paths.

The month cache stopped being local

For most of this series, the KV cache lived in one process on one machine.

That changes the shape of the system.

The same month also brought the opposite move in the engine world.

Taken together, these changes show that the system is being rebuilt around fewer, better-understood paths, with cache and routing treated as first-class concerns.

Peer-to-peer KV transfer in LMCache

LMCache v0.5.0 introduced full peer-to-peer support for multiprocess mode.

Why would a team want this?

A useful way to frame the decision is as a cost comparison per request.

The break-even point is the important number.

Peer transfer also raises a trust and correctness question.

The TensorRT backend sunset

TensorRT-LLM's v1.3.0rc20 release candidate, dated June 30, begins with a notice in bold: this release candidate will be the last one supporting the TensorRT backend, and the backend will be removed in the next version.

Backends in inference engines often exist for historical reasons.

The practical response is to inventory which backend each deployment uses, and to set a migration deadline before the removal lands.

Read the notice alongside the candidate's other changes.

MoE load balancing

SGLang v0.5.14 highlighted two MoE load-balancing methods, Waterfill and LPLB.

Load balancing can happen in two places.

The measurement that matters is the spread of work across GPUs at each step, not just the average.

The same logic applies to vLLM v0.23.0's work on DeepSeek V4. The release added expert parallel load balancing for its Mega-MoE path, along with a TRTLLM-gen attention kernel and sparse multi-head latent attention metadata that no longer depends on the DeepSeek V3.2 path.

May 2026 KV offload meets hybrid memory, speculation v2 by default, DeepSeek V4 across engines, KV cache quantization in LMDeploy

Summary: May was the month the memory system became a shared concern across engines.

When memory management becomes a product feature

Through March and April, most engine work aimed at speed or at keeping a single feature from breaking others.

That change follows from the models.

The practical result is that teams now have to choose a memory configuration, not just a model and a batch size.

KV offload meets the hybrid memory allocator

vLLM v0.21.0 says its KV offloading subsystem now integrates with the Hybrid Memory Allocator, including scheduler-side support for sliding-window groups and full HMA enablement.

Offloading adds a second dimension.

The mental model is a budget with two layers.

A useful check for a team is to measure how much of the GPU cache is held by sliding-window layers versus full-attention layers in their model.

Speculation that respects the thinking budget

Reasoning models often run with a budget on how many thinking tokens they may spend before answering.

The lesson generalizes.

SGLang's v0.5.11 release made speculative decoding V2 the default.

For a team serving a reasoning product, the checks are concrete.

What vLLM shipped this month

vLLM had four releases in May.

The v0.21.0 notes contain two changes that will affect builds and dependencies.

The same release added a TOKENSPEED_MLA attention backend on Blackwell for DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi-K2.5. Backends are how the engine selects a kernel for a given hardware and model combination, so a new backend is a new code path that needs its own tests.

v0.22.0 moved DeepSeek V4 into a dedicated package, added NVFP4 fused mixture-of-experts support, full and piecewise CUDA graphs, and multi-token prediction speculation for that model.

DeepSeek V4 across the engines

DeepSeek V4 moved through the engines in May, and the pattern is worth studying.

Three engines supporting one model within two months is a sign that the model's features are now well understood.

SGLang's v0.5.12 notes also describe HiSparse, a mechanism for offloading inactive KV cache to CPU memory, and a later addition of hierarchical caching under a unified radix tree.

The W4A4 MegaMoE kernels in the same release are also notable.

KV cache quantization in LMDeploy

LMDeploy v0.13.0 on May 12 added TurboQuant support for KV cache quantization, with a quant policy value of 42 in its notes.

The payoff is capacity.

LMDeploy's release also integrated cuBLAS grouped batched GEMM for Qwen 3.5 mixture-of-experts inference on Blackwell, and added a refactored tool parser for its API server.

For evaluation, the test is long-output fidelity.

Decode-side caching for disaggregated serving

SGLang v0.5.11 made decode-side prefix caching work under prefill-decode disaggregation.

The reason this matters is that disaggregation separated the phases and, in doing so, broke the locality that prefix caching depended on.

This is a good example of the pattern from earlier months: a feature that solves one problem creates a second problem, and the next release solves that one.

SGLang v0.5.11 also made CUDA 13 and PyTorch 2.11 the default, following the same base shift that vLLM made in April.

Ollama adds a llama.cpp engine

Ollama v0.30.0 on May 13 said it uses llama.cpp to improve compatibility and performance, and that this augments the MLX engine on Apple Silicon.

Two engines inside one local runner is a meaningful design choice.

The lowercasing change is a small reminder of a larger issue.

The GGUF support also matters for the series.

LMCache, Mooncake, and the storage backends

LMCache v0.4.5 on May 15 made the CUDA 13 wheel the default and added several integrations: DeepSeek V4 support on the multiprocess path, a TensorRT-LLM integration, and a new multiprocess connector snapshot for vLLM 0.20.1. v0.4.6 on May 29 followed.

Mooncake v0.3.11 on May 21 added an eviction policy for its bucket storage backend, with batch notification to the master service, and support for duration units in master time-to-live flags.

A cache store that lives across many machines has a consistency problem that a single engine does not.

llm-d v0.7.0 on May 12 has the most operationally important note of the month for cluster teams.

TensorRT-LLM: prefix caching for hybrid models

TensorRT-LLM's candidates through May carried a notable entry: prefix caching for Mamba hybrid models, including Qwen3.5 and Nemotron Super V3. In March, vLLM's block-aligned caching for Mamba states was the fix for hybrid models in that engine.

The release candidate line also added Wan 2.2 image-to-video support and an encode fast path for encoder-only models, which shows the runtime serving a wider range of model types than text generation.

For teams that pin TensorRT-LLM, the advice is the same as in earlier months: keep a candidate in staging, replay traffic against it, and promote only after the tests pass on the stable release.

April 2026 zero-bubble speculation, CPU KV offload with pluggable policies, Gemma 4 and DeepSeek V4 on day zero, partial failure tolerance

Summary: April was the month the stack started absorbing whole new model families and surviving failures.

The month the engine learned to lose a GPU

Most inference engineering writing assumes that every GPU in the deployment stays healthy.

SGLang's v0.5.10 entry on elastic expert parallelism addresses exactly that case.

Partial failure tolerance is also a cost decision.

Zero-bubble overlap for speculative decoding

Theme 1: stop the GPU from waiting for the CPU

To see why asynchronous scheduling matters, you need the basic loop of an LLM server.

Asynchronous scheduling overlaps the two.

Overlapping is hard when the next step depends on the result of the current one.

Check it on your own system: profile one decode step and measure the gap between the end of one GPU kernel batch and the start of the next. If the gap is a meaningful fraction of the step, asynchronous scheduling is directly relevant. If the gap is tiny, the feature will barely change your numbers.

Theme 2: send each request where its cache already lives

The SGLang gateway release is a clean example of routing treated as a cache problem.

Cache-aware routing fixes this by tracking which prefixes each worker holds and sending requests accordingly.

Routing policyCache hit behaviorMain cost
Round robinHit rate falls as worker count grows, because prefixes are duplicatedWasted prefill
Least loadedBalances queues but ignores which worker holds the prefixLower reuse on shared prompts
Consistent hashing on prefixSame prefix goes to the same worker, with no tracking stateUneven load if one prefix is very popular
Cache-aware, radix treeRoutes to the worker holding the longest matching prefixRouter must track worker caches, and that view can go stale

The stale-view problem deserves attention.

Theme 3: long context forces new parallelism

The SGLang chunked pipeline result and the vLLM pipeline work belong together.

Linear scaling is a strong claim, and it depends on the workload.

Concepts you need for this series

Every month leans on the same small set of ideas.

Prefill and decode

An LLM request has two phases.

The metrics follow the phases.

KV cache and PagedAttention

The KV cache stores the keys and values for every token in the context, so decode does not recompute attention over the whole history at each step.

PagedAttention, introduced by Woosuk Kwon and colleagues in "Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention" (arXiv 2309.06180), splits the cache into fixed-size blocks managed like virtual memory pages.

Continuous batching

Static batching waits for a full batch to finish before starting another, so short requests sit idle behind long ones.

Prefix caching

When many requests share a prefix, such as a long system prompt or a retrieved document, the KV cache for that prefix can be computed once and reused.

Agent workloads fit well.

Speculative decoding

Decode is sequential, so each output token normally costs one full pass through the large model.

Disaggregation

Disaggregated serving runs prefill and decode, or other stages such as the vision encoder, on separate workers.

KV cache tiers and offload

GPU memory is the scarcest resource, so the cache does not have to stay there.

A worked memory budget

Concepts stick better with numbers, so take a simple budget.

That arithmetic explains almost every design choice in this series.

Two details change the arithmetic.

Its v0.5.0 release on July 1 added support for MiniCPM-Llama3-V 2.5 and llava in its PyTorch engine, and the 2024 line made LMDeploy a multi-engine project: a turbomind engine for speed and a PyTorch engine for coverage.

Ray: data pipelines and the serving stack

Ray shipped 23 releases in 2024, from 2.9.1 on January 19 to 2.40.0 on December 4. The 2.40.0 release added a Hudi reader to Ray Data and improved memory accounting for pandas blocks.

Batch inference is where memory accounting matters most, because a pipeline that holds many decoded blocks at once can run out of memory long after its first batch succeeded.

What 2024 teaches an operator

  1. Test batching changes against your own load.
  2. Wait for the first patch on a major release.
  3. Read licence changes as operational changes.
  4. Pin local runners.
  5. Check day-one model support against the model's real architecture.

A first release is a statement of intent.

vLLM: from first release to the v0.2 line

vLLM's first tagged release is v0.1.0 on June 20, 2023. The early 0.1 series ran through the summer and early autumn, with patches on June 22, July 5, August 2, August 25, September 7, September 8, and September 11. Each patch was small, and together they show a project in a fast loop, shipping a fix within days of finding a bug.

v0.2.0 on September 28 is the release to read for 2023. Its notes list up to 60 percent performance improvement from optimizing de-tokenization and the sampler, initial support for AWQ quantization without optimized kernels yet, support for RoPE scaling and the LongChat model, and support for Mistral-7B.

AWQ, the activation-aware weight quantization method, arrived in vLLM in this release without optimized kernels.

The later 2.x patches through December, including v0.2.1.post1 on October 17 and v0.2.6 on December 17, continued the pattern of frequent small releases.

Ollama: the first builds and the first Linux GPU

Ollama's first tagged release in this data is v0.0.1 on July 8, 2023, and the next few weeks brought v0.0.2 through v0.0.21 at a pace of several builds a week.

The notes for that Linux release also mention that the tool would run on cloud servers.

Ollama's 2023 history ends with v0.1.17 on December 19 after 39 releases in the year.

Text Generation Inference: v1.0 and a licence change

Text Generation Inference shipped 32 releases in 2023, from v0.2.0 on February 3 to v1.3.4 on December 22. The release history has a clear turning point.

For a technical reader, the licence change is also a reminder to read the licence before the code.

TGI's 2023 releases also show a steady stream of performance and model-support changes between v0.5 and v1.0. The project was at the center of the open serving discussion that year, and its licence decision shaped where teams went next.

It is worth being precise about what a 2023 TGI deployment looked like.

TensorRT-LLM: a first public release

TensorRT-LLM's first release in this data is v0.5.0 on October 19, 2023, with a blank body in the notes.

The late-2023 TensorRT-LLM releases mattered because they opened a path that later became central.

Its early releases followed a steady rhythm of about one every two weeks, with v0.0.5 on August 15 and v0.0.10 on September 26 among the milestones.

The 2023 LMDeploy history shows a project starting on a broad base.
