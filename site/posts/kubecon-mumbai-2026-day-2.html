KubeCon India 2026, Mumbai — day 2.

Day 2 is the closing day of KubeCon + CloudNativeCon India 2026. This is the field report and session index: what the morning keynotes said about banking, sovereignty, and the human cost of security; the threads that ran through the afternoon tracks; and a curated index of every breakout I tracked. Where a speaker uploaded their slides, the session links to a full deck-by-deck deep dive (marked deck) — 12 of them this day, published through the series. Sessions without public slides are listed as accurate pointers (title, speaker, room) so the index stays complete and honest.

If Day 1 was the scale & sovereignty day, Day 2 was the make-it-real day. The keynotes traded grand vision for operational truth — running emergency-services (911) workloads on a shared cluster, automating RBI compliance as code, an NBFC turning itself into a full bank on Kubernetes, SBI's sovereign cloud serving hundreds of millions. The afternoon tracks were unusually hands-on: debugging 60 idle GPUs, ditching kube-proxy at 10M sessions/day, two-node edge storage, sandboxing LLMs, zero-trust for fintech with Cilium. Less "here's the future," more "here's the bug and how we fixed it."

How day 2 actually felt

The closing day of a KubeCon has a particular texture: slightly fewer people (some fly out early), but the ones who stay are the operators — the people who came for the deep technical tracks, not the expo. Registration and the cloakroom opened early in the Lotus Foyer; the keynote relay started 9:45am in Jasmine 2 and, as on Day 1, ran as a series of short, sharp talks rather than one long set piece.

What stood out was how Indian the keynote line-up was — not as a token, but as the actual centre of gravity. Motorola Solutions on emergency-call workloads, Nirmata + Razorpay on Reserve Bank of India compliance-as-code, Slice on going from NBFC to bank, SBI on a sovereign cloud, a whole panel on the Indian banking sector's cloud-native shift. The subtext: regulated, high-stakes, population-scale systems are where this community is doing its most interesting work, and they're increasingly built here.

The afternoon fractured into the usual parallel rooms (Jasmine 2, Lotus 1–3, rooms 204/205), and that's where the index below lives — AI serving and agents in one stream, networking and the Linux kernel in another, platform/developer-experience in a third, core-Kubernetes and reliability in a fourth, with edge, storage, and security woven through. The day closed with lightning talks, an OpenStack/StarlingX meetup, and the slow goodbye of a conference winding down.

The keynotes — what the morning signalled

After "Welcome Back" and opening remarks, the relay leaned hard into regulated, high-stakes infrastructure. The ones that set the tone:

  • From Kubernetes Learning to Community Leadership (Lavanya Anbalagan, MatrixKube) — a pipeline-of-people message: how the next generation of cloud-native engineers gets built, and why community leadership matters as much as code.
  • Running 911 & Mission-Critical Workloads on Shared Kubernetes Platforms (Manoj K R, Motorola Solutions) — when a dropped packet is a dropped emergency call, how do you safely co-tenant life-critical workloads on a shared platform? Multi-tenancy with the stakes turned all the way up.
  • The Human Cost of Security in the AI Era (Sonali Srivastava, Improving) — a deliberately non-technical keynote about burnout, alert fatigue, and the people behind the dashboards as AI multiplies the security surface.
  • Automating RBI Compliance With Unified Policy-as-Code (Jim Bugwadia, Nirmata & Ashwath Kumar, Razorpay) — turning Reserve Bank of India regulatory requirements into Kyverno-style policy that's enforced automatically rather than audited after the fact. The clearest "Day 1 themes, now in production" moment.
  • The Vertically Integrated Bank (Faeka Ansari, Slice Financial Bank) — scaling from NBFC to a full integrated bank on Kubernetes, developer-experience first.
  • SBI Meghdoot — Modern Sovereign Cloud (Rajesh Singh, SBI) — the State Bank of India's own cloud, serving a customer base the size of a large country, built for sovereignty.
  • One Deploy Plane, Every Runtime (Aqeel Nazeer, Shopify) — how Shopify generalised its cloud-native deploy plane beyond Kubernetes, so one control surface drives many runtimes.

The through-line from Day 1's "AI is the gravity well" shifted to something more grounded: regulation and trust are the constraints that actually shape these systems. Policy-as-code, sovereignty, multi-tenancy under life-or-death SLAs, and the human toll of running it all — that's the closing day's thesis. Hold that up to the afternoon talks and they snap into focus.

Day 2 takeaways

  • Regulation is now a first-class design input. RBI compliance-as-code, sovereign clouds, and 911-grade multi-tenancy were keynote material — not edge cases.
  • The AI conversation got operational. Less "agents will change everything," more gateways, GPU queueing, inference metrics, sandboxes, and isolation.
  • Networking went deep. Past kube-proxy, into Cilium/eBPF, air-gap, and netlink — the unglamorous layer that decides whether scale actually works.
  • The platform wants to disappear. Crossplane/Score, buildpacks, invisible platforms, and a CNCF DevEx roadmap all point the same way: less YAML in front of developers.
  • India is setting the agenda. Banking, payments, and public-scale systems anchored the day — the centre of gravity, not a regional sidebar.

FAQ

Why do only some sessions have full deep dives?

Because deep dives are built from the speakers' uploaded slide decks. For Day 2, 12 sessions published public decks on the schedule, so those get full deck-by-deck write-ups. Sessions without public slides are listed as accurate pointers (title, speaker, room) rather than invented summaries.

How is this different from the Day 1 series?

Day 1 had a deck for every one of its 17 talks, so all 17 got deep dives. Day 2's deck coverage is partial, so the hub doubles as an honest index: deep dives where there are slides, pointers where there aren't.

Is the "When the Edge Can't Afford a Third Node" talk related to Day 1's Rook session?

Yes — directly. The Day 1 Rook maintainers' talk flagged two-node clusters and pointed to this dedicated follow-up. The Day 2 deep dive picks up exactly there: floating mon, Pacemaker fencing, and surviving split-brain on two nodes.
