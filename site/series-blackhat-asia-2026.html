Briefings — Deep Dives.

The Black Hat Asia 2026 briefings (Marina Bay Sands, Singapore), rebuilt talk by talk from the speakers' published decks and white papers. Analysis-first: each post explains the research question, the core idea, and what defenders and builders should change. It deliberately leaves out indicators and step-by-step reproduction, so follow the official slides for that. Newest posts appear at the top.

18

Graph-Aware LLM for Windows Logons (Shusei Tomonaga)

Millions of Windows logon events become a small user-host graph, ranked by PageRank and an HMM, then investigated by a guarded closed-loop LLM agent. Precision 0.90, recall 0.931, and a real intrusion triaged in 195 seconds.

intermediate 10 min 17

Hack the Source, Of the Source — Package Registries as Supply Chain (Tsi-Lin Ng)

Instead of publishing a malicious package, what if you compromise the registry's own backend logic? Disclosed, fixed bugs across LuaRocks, JuliaHub, NuGet, and CocoaPods show manifest generation, git-based version resolution, metadata-file selection, and account verification are all part of the trust boundary.

advanced 11 min 16

One Char to Rule Them All — The SHAR DNS Attack (Fasheng Miao)

Two RFCs disagree about which characters a domain name may contain, and many real implementations silently drop queries with disallowed characters instead of erroring. That silence turns a sub-100ms cache-poisoning race into a multi-second window — reviving brute-force poisoning thought dead since 2008, at internet scale.

advanced 11 min 15

Fortifying the Foundation — LLM Differential Testing for Ethereum (Jie Ma, Ningyu He et al.)

OpDiffer and APIDiffer use LLMs to generate spec-valid test cases and judge real bugs vs. benign differences across Ethereum's diverse client implementations — 98 real bugs found across all 11 mainstream clients, including a spec-level error.

advanced 13 min 14

We'll Eat Your Serial for Breakfast (Dashevskyi, La Spina)

Serial-to-IP converters bridge decades-old ICS equipment onto IP networks and are already a real attack target (Sandworm, Industroyer, the 2025 Poland grid attack). They're ordinary embedded Linux boxes — new critical RCE and auth-bypass flaws mean full takeover, not just DoS.

intermediate 12 min 13

Qualcomm BootROM: A Journey Through Sahara (Kozlov, Anufrienko)

A high-level look at layered boot security on Qualcomm's MDM9207 IoT modem chipset (CVE-2026-25262): four stacked trust guarantees, tested one by one, and why "needs physical access" still matters for field-deployed IoT hardware.

advanced 8 min 12

Payload Compromised — Full Key Recovery in Rocket.Chat E2EE (Hayato Kimura et al.)

A custom password generator whose toLowerCase() collapses 62 chars to 36 makes the E2EE root password brute-forceable; with AES-CBC (no integrity), no key rotation, and TOFU keys, a malicious server reads and forges every message. ProVerif + code review + OSINT.

advanced 9 min 11

PhantomRPC — Discovering New RPC-Impersonation LPEs (Kaspersky)

The Windows RPC runtime can't verify a server is legitimate, so a low-priv process with SeImpersonatePrivilege can register a phantom server and impersonate SYSTEM — a "Potato"-class LPE. The novel part: an ETW-driven pipeline that auto-discovers new triggers.

advanced 9 min 10

Discovering React2Shell (Lachlan Davidson)

How CVE-2025-55182 — the React Server Components / "Flight" deserialization RCE attacked over a billion times in week one — works: a rich JS wire format that can reference built-ins, plus await's recursive resolution of any object with a then method.

advanced 9 min 09

Breaking Hybrid Boundaries Across Azure and Windows (Kalendarov, Zamir)

Windows Admin Center is the bridge between Azure and on-prem — and five CVEs (writable ProgramData LPE, PoP-token-mixing tenant-wide RCE, unvalidated attestation, flow-redirection/XSS) chain into bidirectional compromise. Treat management tools as Tier Zero.

advanced 8 min 08

Your Number Is Up — WhatsApp E2EE Client Attacks (Tal Be'ery)

E2EE moves the attack surface from server to client: silent online-status recon, per-device OS fingerprinting, and pinpointed exploit delivery to one device via the Sesame multi-device protocol — the recon+delivery half of a targeted 0-click kill-chain.

intermediate 10 min 07

The Gift That Keeps On Giving — Bypassing Auth-Reflection Mitigations (Guillaume André)

A generic patch-bypass methodology turns one Windows auth-reflection fix into a chain of SYSTEM-shell CVEs. The clever core: a Unicode homoglyph makes a DNS record and an SPN collide under AD's sort keys, rebuilding Kerberos coercion.

expert 11 min 06

Breaking the Illusion of Key Zeroization (Toyofumi Sawa, Kuniyasu Suzaki)

FPGA-based live memory scanning shows AES keys survive abnormal termination, get duplicated into kernel memory by vector-register context switches and core dumps, and persist across reboots. Zeroization is a whole-stack problem.

advanced 10 min 05

The Dark Side of Autonomy — Exploiting DFIR Agents (Yusuke Nakajima)

An autonomous DFIR agent (LLM + Velociraptor over MCP) can be turned against the investigation by instructions planted in the artifacts it collects — Hide, Mislead, Exploit. Strict structured output drops the attack to ~0%.

advanced 10 min 04

IntentGuard — Semantic Validation of LLM-Generated IaC (Anna Bacher, Chris Wysopal)

A hidden prompt injection in a public module can make an LLM emit a "reasonable-looking" insecure cloud config that passes every static scanner. IntentGuard encodes infrastructure into description logic and flags where the config violates intent.

advanced 10 min 03

AirSnitch — Breaking Wi-Fi Client Isolation (Mathy Vanhoef et al.)

The guest-network isolation feature is enforced one layer at a time, so an attacker who moves between the Wi-Fi, IP, and Ethernet layers walks around it — into full two-way MitM. Every tested device was vulnerable.

advanced 13 min 02

Cast Attack — "Ghost Bits" in Java (Xinyu Bai, Zhihui Chen)

Casting a 16-bit char to an 8-bit byte drops the high byte, so a Unicode character becomes an attacker-chosen ASCII byte. A WAF sees safe Unicode; the parser sees the payload. The bug class, its many faces, and the fix.

advanced 13 min 01

Quantum Risk & Post-Quantum Crypto — A Roadmap (JP Aumasson)

What a quantum computer breaks, why nobody knows when, what NIST PQC costs in bytes, what's deployable today, and a four-phase quantum-risk management loop.
